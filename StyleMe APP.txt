===========================

Current baseline, revised 2026-10-10. This blueprint describes the product
StyleMe is building, from the first real release to the long-term vision.
It is consistent with the documentation in docs/, which holds the detailed
specifications:

  docs/BRD.md             business requirements
  docs/MVP.md             first release scope and acceptance criteria

  docs/ARCHITECTURE.md    architecture

  docs/API.md             API contract
  docs/AI.md              recommendation engine and AI design
  docs/SECURITY.md        security and privacy
  docs/TESTING.md         tests and evaluation
  docs/DEPLOYMENT.md      environments, deployment and operations
  docs/CONTRIBUTING.md    workflow and content review
  docs/ImplementationPlan.md   milestones, estimates and the decision
                               register (§8) — the single list of
                               decisions (D-01 … D-85)

There is no demo release. Every release is a real product used by real
people.


1. PRODUCT OVERVIEW
-------------------
StyleMe is a personal styling product for users in India that grows into
a styling-and-shopping platform.

First release (Stage 1 — Personal Styling):

Create Account
    ↓
Create Style Profile
(department, sizes and fit per category, liked/disliked colours, contrast,
silhouette, coverage, comfort, fabrics, styles, occasions)
    ↓
Optional Self-Reported Appearance Signals
(skin-tone range, undertone, proportions — switchable and deletable)
    ↓
Ask for Looks (occasion, optional style, suggested or explore palette)
    ↓
Deterministic Look Generation
(templates → palettes → styling, fit and fabric rules → look specification)
    ↓
Up to 5 Distinct Looks
(colour swatches, look specification per slot, size note, search phrase)
    ↓
"Why This Works" Explanation
    ↓
Alternatives + Palette Exploration + Adjust Preferences
    ↓
Save / Revisit / Rate / Delete Looks
    ↓
(Optional) Shortlist: add items you found, with prices you enter,
to a saved look's slots
    ↓
Total of Your Prices vs Your Budget
("entered by you — not verified by StyleMe; not a checkout price")
    ↓
Delete Account at Any Time

Long-term vision, in stages (§2):
- verified real products from authorised retailers, matched to looks;
- budgets on verified prices, product quality evidence, comparison where
  permitted;
- optional weather-aware suggestions;
- if approved later: photo-based appearance analysis, 3D avatar, Design
  Studio, sketch-to-design, fashion chatbot, tailor specification PDF;
- virtual try-on only if decision D-48 allows it.

Priorities, in order:
1. user value and recommendation quality;
2. correctness, trust and honest information;
3. legal permissions, privacy and security;
4. a feasible first release;
5. low operating costs;
6. extensibility.


2. STAGES
---------
Stage 1 — Personal Styling (first real release)
  Accounts, profile, self-reported signals, style knowledge base,
  deterministic looks, palettes, alternatives, size notes, search phrases,
  saved looks, feedback, account deletion.
  Shortlist and budget (D-77 = Option B, approved 2026-10-10): users add
  items they found themselves, with prices they enter; the budget engine
  totals those prices against their budget. Items, prices and totals are
  always labelled as entered by the user and not verified by StyleMe. No
  retailer approval is needed for this.
  Released as a closed beta for invited users (invite-only sign-up,
  D-63). Public launch needs the legal review D-74 recorded complete and
  self-service email password reset working (HC-2, D-84); the exact
  blocker list is in docs/ImplementationPlan.md §11.
  Release blockers: content set C1 approved; hosting decided at the
  hosting checkpoint (D-81). Public launch also needs D-74.

Product layer
  Verified real products, matching to look slots, budgets on verified
  prices, quality evidence, comparison where permitted.
  Code can be built and tested on fixtures at any time, behind a flag.
  Release blockers: an authorised source (D-43 … D-45), legal review
  (D-74), disclosures (D-46).
  Released in Stage 3.

Stage 2 — Expanded Styling
  The full styling library C2 (100+ palettes, 100+ templates, Dresses
  and 8–12 categories) — a release requirement; optional weather
  (approved; provider chosen when S2.2 starts, D-60).

Stage 3 — Authorised Shopping
  Product layer release; more retailers as access is granted.

Deferred (D-76, pending founder decision)
  Photo-based appearance analysis, 3D avatar, Design Studio,
  sketch-to-design, fashion chatbot, tailor PDF.

Future (D-48, pending founder decision)
  Generative virtual try-on.

Styling content (D-57):
  Product requirement: at least 100 colour palettes and at least 100
             outfit templates (planning range 100–150), 8–12 categories
             and a comprehensive rule library — content set C2,
             completed and approved before the Stage 2 release.
  Stage 1 release set (C1): 12–20 palettes, about 20 templates, rules
             covering coordination, occasions, preferences, fit, sizes,
             budget where it applies, and alternatives.
  Development seed (C0, temporary): at least 6 palettes and 6
             templates, used only to build and test the engine in S1.4.
             Not a release target.
  Product catalogue (product layer): 500–1,000 human-reviewed listings.


3. PRINCIPLES AND BUSINESS RULES
--------------------------------
KEEP THE CORE DETERMINISTIC.
KEEP AI OPTIONAL AND ISOLATED.
NEVER PRESENT UNVERIFIED INFORMATION AS VERIFIED.
VALIDATE ALL INPUT AND ALL AI OUTPUT.
COLLECT ONLY WHAT A FEATURE USES; LET USERS DELETE IT.
KEEP SECRETS SERVER-SIDE AND ENVIRONMENTS SEPARATE.
KEEP FRONTEND AND BACKEND CONTRACTS SHARED.
ADD INFRASTRUCTURE ONLY FOR A CONCRETE NEED.

Business rules:
1.  Looks, ranking, explanations, size notes and budgets are code. No core
    feature depends on external AI.
2.  AI never invents or changes prices, availability, products or quality.
3.  Budget totals are computed in code from user-entered prices (Stage 1)
    or verified prices (product layer), never combined, labelled with
    their basis, never presented as checkout prices. User-entered items
    and prices are labelled "not verified by StyleMe".
4.  Optional enhancements fail into the core result; core failures give a
    clear error, never a fake answer.
5.  Nothing is fabricated. Missing values are shown as "Not available".
6.  Every verified price shows when it was verified, and the retailer's
    page is final. Prices the user enters are labelled as theirs and not
    verified by StyleMe.
7.  Only verified real products are shown as products. Test fixtures
    never exist in production.
8.  Retailer data only through permitted methods. No scraping.
9.  No guarantee that a product recreates a look.
10. Products with serious, evidenced quality problems are not recommended.
11. Suitability and evidenced quality always outrank commercial
    considerations. Affiliate monetisation is off by default and never
    affects ranking or quality.
12. Tracked links carry a disclosure (D-46).
13. Styling signals are suggestions, never restrictions; nothing is
    called objectively unsuitable for a body or skin tone.
14. Body shape never determines size.
15. Weather is optional; precise location is never stored.
16. Personal photographs, measurements and unnecessary personal
    information are never sent to external AI providers, unless a future
    explicit founder decision, a privacy review and user consent
    authorise the specific processing.
17. Users can always explore any colour, fit or style.
18. Users can delete their data and their account.


4. DATA HONESTY MODEL
---------------------
Every piece of information shown belongs to one class and is labelled:

  User-provided  profile, sizes, signals, budget; shortlist items and
                 prices the user enters (labelled "not verified by
                 StyleMe")
  Verified       real product facts from a permitted source, with source
                 and "as of" time (product layer)
  Derived        looks, explanations, size notes and totals computed by
                 StyleMe (a total of user-entered prices keeps the "not
                 verified by StyleMe" label)
  Estimated      model estimates with confidence (deferred features only)
  Unavailable    shown as "Not available"

A verified real product exists on a retailer's site and was recorded from
a permitted source with evidence:
  affiliate_api / partner_feed : source method, sync job, fetch time,
                                 source record ID, recorded permissions
  manual_curation              : source URL, capture time, curator,
                                 review date, criteria version, status

When no product data exists, the look is still shown as a complete styling
suggestion with a generic search phrase. StyleMe never fills the gap with
placeholders, test data or a poorer match.

Test fixtures (isFixture: true) exist only in local, CI and staging
databases. Production refuses to start if one is present; ingestion
rejects them; seed scripts refuse to run in production.

Style illustrations are labelled "Style illustration — not a product" and
have a recorded source and licence (D-65); otherwise a labelled swatch is
shown.

8. AUTHENTICATION
-----------------
Browser → Auth.js (encrypted session cookie) → Next.js server (BFF)
→ Express API (access token) → MongoDB

- Passwords: at least 8 characters, argon2id hashes only.
- Login: the API verifies the hash, creates a server-side session
  record and issues a JWT (HS256, 7 days, carrying tokenVersion and the
  session ID). Every API request checks signature, expiry, isActive,
  tokenVersion and the session record; a JWT alone is never enough.
- The token lives inside the encrypted session cookie (HttpOnly, Secure
  in staging and production, SameSite=Lax, host-only) and never reaches
  browser JavaScript. State-changing requests check Origin.
- JWT and Auth.js session both expire 7 days after login; the session
  is not extended past the token. Logout calls POST /v1/auth/logout,
  which deletes that session record (so a copied cookie stops working),
  then clears the cookie (D-07). Password change, reset and the admin
  script delete all session records and increment tokenVersion;
  isActive false blocks login.
- Generic responses for login, registration and reset requests.
- Reset links carry the token in the URL fragment; the reset page
  removes it, sends Referrer-Policy: no-referrer and loads no
  third-party resources; tokens never reach logs or third parties.
- Password change (D-83): current password required; all tokens
  revoked; the current session gets a new token.
- Password recovery (D-83):
  Closed beta — admin-assisted: after verifying the request through a
  channel tied to the account, an admin script issues a single-use
  reset code (>= 128 bits, valid 60 minutes, stored only as a hash),
  shown once for out-of-band delivery. The user redeems it and sets
  the new password; the admin never sees or chooses it.
  Public launch — self-service email reset is required: generic
  responses, single-use links valid 30 minutes, stored only as hashes,
  rate-limited. Email provider chosen at HC-2 (D-84).
  Every reset increments tokenVersion and deletes other reset tokens.
- Account deletion requires the password. It first writes a minimal
  deletion tombstone to a separate store (D-85), then deletes all user
  data from the live database immediately. Copies in backups are not
  edited; they expire under the backup retention period (D-79, 30 days
  proposed, not yet approved). If the tombstone can't be written,
  nothing is deleted.
- Closed beta: while PUBLIC_SIGNUP_ENABLED is false, only emails on the
  admin-managed invite allow-list (betaInvites) can register. It stays
  false until every public-launch blocker in docs/ImplementationPlan.md
  §11 is met, including D-74 recorded complete.


9. REQUEST ARCHITECTURE AND BACKEND PATTERN
-------------------------------------------
Route → Authentication → Validation (Zod) → Controller (HTTP only)
→ Service (business logic) → Model / Integration → Response

Response envelope:
  { "success": true, "data": {}, "warnings": [] }
  { "success": false, "error": { "code": "...", "message": "..." } }

Errors never expose stack traces, secrets, database details or internals.
External services are reached only through src/integrations/.

  profiles            department, sizes and fit per

  aiCache             only if AI wording is enabled; no personal data
  shortlistItems      the user's own finds: name, priceEntered (₹),
                      optional https URL (never fetched), optional note,
                      optional outfitId + slotId; priceBasis user_entered;
                      no retailer, stock, rating, image or quality fields

Shortlist and budget rules (Stage 1):
- Every item, price and total is labelled: "Added by you — not verified
  by StyleMe", "Price entered by you — not verified by StyleMe", "Total
  of prices you entered — not verified by StyleMe; not a checkout price".
- StyleMe never fetches, previews or modifies the link, and derives no
  retailer name, logo, image, stock, rating or quality from it.
- A saved look's price summary is computed on read: items total, priced
  slots of total slots, budget remaining, within / over / no budget.
  Example: ₹699 + ₹999 + ₹699 = ₹2,397 for 3 of 3 slots; budget ₹2,500
  → within budget, ₹103 remaining.
- Budget never filters generated looks, which carry no prices.
- Limits (D-80): name ≤ 120 characters, note ≤ 300, URL ≤ 2,048 and
  https only, price > 0 and ≤ ₹10,00,000; 200 items per user.
- Attachment: outfitId and slotId are set together or both left empty.
  Unattached items (both empty) are allowed in any number up to the
  200-item limit. Attached items are unique per {outfitId, slotId}
  (D-82), enforced by a partial unique index that covers only attached
  items. Deleting a look clears both fields on its items.

Product layer:
  retailers           retailerId, domains, accessLevel (A–D),
                      sourceMethod, status, recorded permissions,
                      disclosure text
  products            verified listings only: provenance, evidence,

  reviewEvidence      curated evidence with source and capture time; no

12. APPEARANCE SIGNALS AND CONFIDENCE
-------------------------------------
Each appearance attribute keeps the value used for recommendations apart
from any model estimate:

  { value, source, userConfirmed, confirmedAt, estimate }

  source = self_reported   Stage 1. The user entered it. No confidence is
                           stored or shown.
  source = photo_estimate  Deferred photo analysis. estimate holds value,
                           the model's confidence and analysisVersion;
                           value stays empty until the user confirms.
  source = user_corrected  The user changed an estimate; the estimate is
                           kept unchanged.

Rules:
- Confirmation never changes the model's confidence and never sets it
  to 1.
- An estimate below the calibrated threshold (D-15) is shown as "not
  sure", never forced into a class.
- Withdrawing photo consent clears photo-derived estimates (D-11).
- Only confirmed values are used, and only while useInRecommendations is
  on.
- Signals are soft styling inputs and never affect size.
- Signals are never sent to an external AI provider.


13. STYLE KNOWLEDGE BASE
------------------------
Four record types in apps/api/src/style-rules/:

  PAL-xxx  palettes: undertone affinity, optional skin-tone range,
           neutrals, accents, combinations with contrast level, prints,
           occasions
  STY-xxx  styling/fit rules: silhouette, fit, neckline, sleeve, length,
           layering, proportion, print scale, combination; soft
           prefer/deprioritise effects with weights
  FAB-xxx  fabric rules: breathability, moisture management, quick-dry,
           warmth, weight, stretch, wrinkle resistance, care; weather
           bands in Stage 2
  OUT-xxx  outfit templates: slots (each with a stable slotId unique
           within the template, e.g. top, accessory-1, accessory-2, and
           a slot category/type), occasions, styles, department,
           compatible palettes, rule references

Every record: id, version, tags, applicable contexts, user-adjustable
preferences, rationale (an effect, never a judgement), limitations,
reviewStatus (draft / reviewed / approved / retired), reviewer, review
date.

A linter (CI and startup) rejects contradictions, duplicates, duplicate
slotIds within a template, unknown
references, missing rationale or limitations, and judgemental language.
Only approved records load. The founder reviews all content
(docs/CONTRIBUTING.md §6).

Rule coverage: coordination across slots (colour compatibility,
formality, print and pattern mixing, proportion balance, footwear and
accessory pairing), occasions, user preferences, fit, sizes (size notes
only), budget where it applies, and meaningfully different alternatives.

Scale: IDs PAL-001 and up; files loaded and indexed once at startup by
occasion, style, department and category; designed for at least 150
palettes and 150 templates without code changes, proven by a scale test
on a synthetic fixture library. A CI coverage report shows thin or empty
occasion × style × department cells and drives content planning.

Content programme (docs/ImplementationPlan.md §5.2): C0 seed (temporary)
→ C1 Stage 1 release set → C2 full library (100+ / 100+, Stage 2 gate).
Each batch: plan from the coverage report → draft (any contributor) →
schema, linter, coverage and scale checks → evaluation set (grown to
cover every occasion × department) → founder approval → release gate.


14. LOOK RECOMMENDATION ENGINE
------------------------------
Request (occasion required; style and paletteMode optional)
  + profile + enabled self-reported signals
    ↓
Compatible templates (occasion, style, department)
    ↓
Palettes: liked colours boosted, disliked excluded, undertone as a soft
signal; "explore" allows any colour not disliked
    ↓
Precedence (hard user constraints filter; the rest apply as soft
weights, in this order):
  1. hard user constraints (disliked colours, avoided fabrics, coverage,
     department; budget only with the product layer)
  2. explicit preferences
  3. approved rules
  4. self-reported signals
  5. weather (Stage 2)
    ↓
Look specification per slot
    ↓
Diversity: unique combination signatures; at most 5 looks
    ↓
Alternatives per slot: 3–5 when available, never padded; each differs
meaningfully and stays coordinated with the rest of the look
    ↓
Explanation from rule rationale and matched attributes, with references
    ↓
[Product layer] eligibility → matching → ranking → look match per slot
    ↓
[Optional, off by default] AI rewording of the explanation
    ↓
Stored as suggested looks → response

- Missing inputs: defaults are applied and listed (DEFAULTS_APPLIED).
- Conflicts: shown; the more restrictive choice applies
  (PREFERENCE_CONFLICT).
- Too few matches: only valid looks are returned, possibly none, with the
  limiting constraint and a suggested relaxation (INSUFFICIENT_MATCHES).
  This is a successful response, not an error.
- Each slot has a generic search phrase (e.g. "navy cotton straight-fit
  kurta") with no link, price or availability.
- Ranking is a weighted sum of rule effects and preference matches, tuned
  on the evaluation set. Affiliate status is never an input.
- If every AI provider fails, nothing above changes: only the optional
  wording step is skipped (AI_ENHANCEMENT_SKIPPED).


15. SIZING
----------
Sizes are per category group, never one universal size:

  Tops (T-shirts, Shirts)      Letter XS–3XL; shirts may use collar size
  Bottoms (Jeans, Trousers)    Waist in inches; optional length
  Ethnic tops (Kurtas, Kurtis) Letter, or numeric where the retailer uses it
  Ethnic sets (Lehengas)       Letter or waist; often semi-stitched
  Free size (Sarees, most accessories)   no size
  Shoes                        UK/India size
  Dresses (Stage 2)            Letter XS–3XL

Stage 1: each slot shows the user's saved size for its category, or asks
for one. Body shape and signals never affect size.

Product layer: retailer size charts only where permitted; optional
self-measured measurements, collected only then and deletable; size
guidance with its basis and confidence, or "Size guidance unavailable —
please choose your size".


16. OPTIONAL AI
---------------
Stage 1 needs no AI provider. The only AI step is optional rewording of
explanations:
- off by default; enabled only if the evaluation set shows clearer
  explanations without loss of accuracy;
- input: rule IDs, rule rationale and matched attribute values only —
  no identity, profile free text, signals, sizes, measurements or photos;
- output validated with Zod; rejected if it mentions anything not in its
  input or contains judgemental language;
- any failure (disabled, timeout, rate limit, quota, invalid output)
  returns the template text with AI_ENHANCEMENT_SKIPPED, never an error;
- cached in aiCache; prompts versioned in the repository.

AI-required deferred features (chatbot, sketch-to-design) return a clear
error when AI is unavailable, never a fake answer.


17. AI SAFETY AND RELIABILITY RULES
-----------------------------------
1.  Never claim exact body measurements without calibration.
2.  Never force a low-confidence classification.
3.  Show the model's confidence for estimates; never invent a confidence.
4.  Let the user correct any estimate.
5.  User-confirmed and self-reported values override model estimates.
6.  AI never invents or changes product prices.
7.  Budgets are validated by code.
8.  All AI output is validated with Zod and business rules.
9.  AI failures produce a deterministic fallback or a clear error.
10. API keys never leave the server.
11. Never fabricate reviews, ratings, prices, availability, products or
    quality claims.
12. Every quality claim cites stored evidence; no evidence means
    "insufficient evidence".
13. Never guarantee that a product will recreate a look.
14. AI never overrides availability, freshness or quality verdicts.
15. Never claim a body shape or skin tone makes an outfit objectively
    unsuitable.

18. PRODUCT LAYER
-----------------
Sourcing
  Only verified real products from retailers at access level A (approved
  API or feed) or B (permitted manual curation). Source methods:
  affiliate_api, partner_feed, manual_curation. No scraping.

Ingestion and validation
  Curated listings go through an import script; automated sources through
  a sync script within the provider's rate limits. A record is rejected
  if: the product URL is outside the retailer's domains; price ≤ 0 or MRP
  below price; a timestamp is in the future; evidence is missing; it is a
  fixture; or the retailer isn't active at level A or B. Missing values
  stay null. Expiring signed feed URLs are never stored.

Freshness (starting thresholds, per provider terms, D-50)
  API data:       stale after 24 h, expired after 72 h
  Manual data:    stale after 7 days, expired after 14 days
  Stale: shown with "Price may have changed". Expired: not recommended.

Eligibility
  Active retailer and listing, verified provenance, not a fixture, in
  stock, user's size in stock (or unknown with a warning), link not
  broken, price not expired, quality verdict not rejected.

Matching
  Deterministic search and ranking against each slot's look
  specification: suitability (look match, size) first, then evidenced
  quality, then price; commission is never an input. Look match is likely / partial / uncertain, never
  guaranteed. No eligible product: "No verified product available" with
  the search phrase.

Budget (pure code)
  total = sum of verified unconditional selling prices (never combined
  with user-entered prices)
  within budget only if total <= budget; remaining = budget − total
  MRP, discounts and known shipping shown separately; totals labelled
  "Estimated items total — not a checkout price"; no over-budget look is
  returned.
  Example: ₹699 + ₹999 + ₹699 = ₹2,397; budget ₹2,500 → ₹103 remaining.

Quality evidence
  Curated evidence (ratings summaries, excerpts where permitted, complaint
  themes, photo-discrepancy notes) with source and capture time. A
  deterministic verdict: acceptable / caution / rejected /
  insufficient_evidence, with confidence (never 1), per-dimension signals,
  reasons and warnings. Seller ratings are a weak signal; StyleMe cannot
  reliably detect fake reviews. Thresholds are proposals until calibrated.

Display
  Retailer, "Price as of <time>", availability, quality verdict, look
  match, link to the listing on an allowed domain. Tracked links show the
  D-46 disclosure. Cross-retailer comparison only where the retailers
  involved permit it. Saved looks re-check matched products when viewed.

No cart, checkout, payments, price-drop alerts, click-out tracking or
live price checks on the request path.

20. WEATHER (STAGE 2, OPTIONAL)
-------------------------------
- Off by default; the user turns it on.
- Location from a chosen city, or device location with permission,
  rounded to about 1 km, used per request, never stored or sent to AI.
- Provider chosen only after commercial terms, pricing and data handling
  are verified (D-60). Open-Meteo's free tier is non-commercial only.
- Comfort bands (starting values): feels-like hot ≥ 32°C, warm 26–31,
  mild 18–25, cool 10–17, cold < 10; humid ≥ 70%; rain likely ≥ 50% or
  ≥ 1 mm; windy ≥ 30 km/h.
- Bands adjust ranking softly through fabric and styling rules; weather
  never excludes a look.
- Unavailable weather: manual entry or continue without it.


21. DEFERRED FEATURES (D-76)

Approved parts of the vision whose stage is not decided. Each is reviewed

Photo-based appearance analysis

  proportions, never used for sizing, no photorealistic reconstruction.

Design Studio

  embroidery, preview, save.

Sketch-to-design
  Sketch upload → AI interpretation into a Zod-validated garment
  specification → user confirmation → generation (provider D-32). AI
  failure gives a clear error.

Fashion chatbot
  Minimised context, no photos, measurements or signals; product facts
  only from stored verified data; AI failure gives a clear error.

Tailor PDF
  A specification PDF from a saved design (PDFKit): garment, fabric,
  colour, neck, sleeve, length, fit, pattern, embroidery, image,
  instructions.

23. SECURITY AND PRIVACY
------------------------
- HTTPS; argon2id; generic login errors; token only in the encrypted
  session cookie; revocation via tokenVersion.
- Authorization on every protected route; all user data isolated.
- Zod validation of every request and AI output.
- Secrets server-side, per environment, never in Git, images or logs; no
  NEXT_PUBLIC_ secrets.
- Rate limits: login 5/min/IP, register 3/min/IP, logout 30/min/user,
  account deletion
  3/hour/user, recommendations 20/min/user, feedback 60/min/user,
  shortlist writes 30/min/user, password change 5/hour/user, reset
  confirm 10/hour/IP, reset request 3/hour/email and 10/hour/IP.
  In-memory and per instance: valid with
  one API instance; more instances need a shared store (HC-1).
- CORS limited to each environment's web origin; Helmet headers.
- Data minimisation: measurements and location are collected only when
  a released feature uses them.
- Shortlist links are https only and never fetched, previewed or
  modified by the server.
- Deletion of individual items and the whole account; out-of-app
  requests within 30 days; deletion is immediate in the live database,
  and backup copies expire under the backup retention period (D-79).
  Deletion tombstones (D-85) make sure a restored backup can't bring a
  deleted account back.
- Logs exclude passwords, tokens, signals, measurements, photos, precise
  location and full request bodies.
- Retailer links restricted to retailer domains; retailer content
  escaped and treated as untrusted.
- India's DPDP Rules, 2025 were notified on 13–14 November 2025; notice
  and consent obligations are reported to start 18 months later, around
  13–14 May 2027. These dates and their applicability are provisional. Exact dates
  and applicability are for the legal review (D-74), which is required
  before public launch and before the product layer. Not legal advice.
Full checklist: docs/SECURITY.md §11.

27. OPERATIONS (D-69, D-79)
---------------------------
- Migrations: versioned, backward-compatible scripts, run before the new
  API version takes traffic.
- Backups: Atlas backups if the tier includes them, otherwise scheduled
  mongodump (weekly and before migrations), 30-day retention (proposed,
  confirmed at HC-1); restore tested in staging before the Stage 1
  closed-beta release.
- Restore (D-85): maintenance mode on (all /v1 endpoints return
  SERVICE_UNAVAILABLE) → check the backup is within tombstone coverage →
  restore into an isolated new database → read all tombstones from the
  separate store → replay deletions → verify zero data for tombstoned
  accounts → switch the API over → maintenance off. Any failure: stay in
  maintenance; the restored database is never served unverified.
- Rollback: redeploy the previous Vercel and Render deployment; restore
  only for data loss.
- Monitoring: structured JSON logs, /health ({ status, version, time }),
  external uptime check with email alerts; sync-failure alerts with the
  product layer.
- Cost: current plan limits and prices for every service are checked and
  recorded before launch; no free tier is assumed to be enough.
